## Amendment 134 (DRAFT 2026-11-26): pose version 4 and the kill channel, joint with EnginePerf (formats and rules; no run)
Ordered by the Overseer as its own joint item, ahead of anomalies and arm E. This amendment registers the two file
formats and the rules of both sides, as amendment 125 did for pose version 3. It registers no run: every run (their
X-only witness, our ITR2-side witness, the joint run) gets its own amendment before it boots. The text of sections 1
to 6 and 8 below is the joint text, draft 7 (`docs/design-notes/2026-11-26-pose-v4-kill-channel-joint-draft.md`),
agreed line by line with EnginePerf; they hold the same text and match it byte for byte.

**The fault it fixes.** Damage and kills the player does in ITR2 never reach X-Ray, which owns who is alive. Today a
puppet the player hurts stays hurt here while X-Ray's creature is unhurt, and a killed puppet's body is moved by the
mod to follow X-Ray's living creature until the game clears it; then the creature is spawned again.

### 1. The pose file, version 4 (ITR2 writes)

    seq,<n>,<utc_ms>,<state>,<level>,<keep|freeze>,<session>,4
    p,<x>,<yfloor>,<z>,<yaw>,<hp>,<stance>
    c,<changer>                      (only in state cross, as today)
    k,<id>,<section>,<total>,<dead>  (0 to 64 lines)
    end,<n>

- The 8th header field is the version, the digit 4. A pose with 7 header fields is version 3 and has no `k` lines.
- A version 3 reader refuses a version 4 pose whole (its header patterns take 6 or 7 fields only): the pose counts as
  torn, nothing is applied, and it is LOST after 5 s. So a version 4 ITR2 cannot drive a version 3 X-Ray. This fails
  closed and is intended; both sides change together.
- The version 4 reader: 7 fields = version 3, and `k` lines in it are not applied and are counted; 8 fields with the
  8th equal to 4 = version 4; any other 8th field, or more than 8 fields, refused whole and counted (`kver`).
- Today's version 3 reader acts on a bare `k,<id>` line (it kills that id). The version 4 reader removes this in every
  version. Until then no version 3 pose may carry a `k` line. Checked in the mod (blob 2c8ad852, `STR.t2Write`): the
  pose body is built from one format string with the lines `seq`, `p`, an optional `c` and `end`; no `k` line can be written.
- `<id>`: X-Ray's id from the snapshot's `o` line. `<section>`: the section name from that same `o` line, copied.
- `<total>`: all damage ITR2 has dealt to this creature in this ITR2 session, as a share of full health, 3 decimals,
  0.000 to 1.000. It never falls within a session.
- `<dead>`: 1 once the puppet has died in ITR2, else 0. Always present.
- The form `k,<id>` with fewer than 5 fields is not valid in any version and is refused.
- A line is written only for an id that was listed in a snapshot of the current X-Ray run id.
- More than 64 creatures with damage: the 64 with the newest change are written; the mod counts the rest (`kdrop`).

### 2. The snapshot (X-Ray writes)

    r,<session>,<pose seq>      at most one line
    k,<id>,<applied>            for every id with applied > 0 that is alive
    k,<id>,<applied>,dead       for 10 s after a death was applied or seen, or while a pose line for it is stale

- After the `o` lines, before `end`; the `r` line before the `k` lines. The `o` line is unchanged.
- The `r` line (EnginePerf's wording): seq of the newest version 4 pose whose k lines X-Ray has processed (also when it
  carried none). Absent until the first such pose of this X-Ray run. Not advanced while the pose is LOST, in a
  crossing, for another level, or refused (torn, kver). On a new session number it carries the new session and that
  pose's seq.
- Processed means: every death in that pose is applied or confirmed in the same step. So a snapshot whose `r` is at or
  past a death's first pose also carries the `dead` line for it, or the kill was refused (and a stale one is answered
  `dead` as well), or it failed and is not confirmed. In the failed case ITR2's 10 s rule ends it: `KILL not applied`,
  and the mod follows X-Ray. An owed part hit is processed but not applied: `r` advances, `applied` stays.
- `r` is the last pose processed before this snapshot was written; pose reads and snapshot writes are separate steps,
  so it can lag the pose file by one read.
- Nothing else in the snapshot says the pose is LOST; an `r` line standing still is the sign.
- Header: `seq,<n>,<t_xray_ms>,<t_track_s>,<level>,<run id>,4`. The 7th field is the version, the digit 4: one bridge
  version for both files. A snapshot with 6 header fields has no `k` lines.
- The mod's reader today takes the first six header fields and ignores line types it does not know, so an older mod is
  not disturbed by a version 4 snapshot.

### 3. What ITR2 does

- Each puppet tick, on the game thread: read the puppet's health. The drop since the value the mod itself last wrote is
  damage dealt in ITR2; it is added to the total as a share of the class's full health.
- Death: health reads 0 on a valid actor. `dead` = 1, and the total includes the drop to 0. From then on the body is
  ITR2's: the mod neither moves nor removes it, and does not spawn that id again while the report is open.
- A puppet that only vanished (fell out of the world, cleared by the game) with health above 0 at its last read is NOT
  reported dead: reporting it would make X-Ray kill a creature the player never killed. It is spawned again, as today.
- Health is read 20 times a second on the mod's own clock, also while X-Ray stalls, so a death is seen before the
  game clears the body.
- Full health of a puppet is its first health read after spawn (the game shows no maximum to scripts).
- Shown health: full health times (the snapshot's health minus `total - applied`), never below 1 % by this arithmetic.
  Written by the mod after an accepted snapshot when it differs from the puppet's health; the value is read back, and
  only a later drop below it is damage. (New code; today the mod never writes puppet health.)
- The 10 s and 60 s rules below run on a clock of accepted snapshots (each counts its real spacing, at most 0.25 s).
- A death's 10 s count only snapshots whose `r` line names our session and a seq at or past the first pose that
  carried the death. No `r` line, another session or an older seq means X-Ray is not reading us: the kill is kept.
- A report retired and later opened again starts at X-Ray's applied count for that id as of the newest snapshot
  (counts of the current session only), so the total never falls below it.
- Under the 64-line limit deaths are written first, then the newest changes.
- A line is dropped when: X-Ray's `dead` line for the id is read; or the id has been absent from the snapshot for 60 s;
  or the id is listed with another section name.
- X-Ray's `dead` line removes the puppet even when our total is 0.

### 4. What X-Ray does (EnginePerf's wording)

- Reads `k` lines only from a whole pose of version 4. Applies nothing while the pose is LOST, in a crossing, or for
  another level. Refuses and counts: id 0, an id that is not a creature or stalker, a bad or negative total, over 64 lines.
- Part hit, creature online and alive, `total - applied` > 0: a near-zero hit with the actor as source, then health
  lowered directly by the difference, stopping at 0.01 unless `dead` is 1; then `applied = total`. A difference
  above 1.0 is cut to 1.0 and counted. A total below `applied` within one session is ignored and counted.
- Offline part hit: owed. `applied` stays, so the snapshot shows less than the total; applied when the creature is
  online again. An owed part is lost when the ITR2 session changes (the new totals start at 0).
- Death: online, `kill` with the actor as killer; offline, the alife object's own kill (no killer can be named); the
  death is confirmed only if the object then reads dead, otherwise it is logged, counted (`kdead_fail`) and not
  confirmed; already dead or released, confirmed dead.
- A falling total with `dead` = 1: the total is ignored and counted, the death is still applied.
- More than 64 `k` lines in a pose: every `k` line of that pose is refused; the rest of the pose is still applied.
  Refused lines count as processed, so `r` advances. (ITR2's writer never writes more than 64.)
- No object at all for the id (released): confirmed dead whatever the `dead` field says.
- A line for a creature already dead in X-Ray refreshes the 10 s hold, so the `dead` answer stays until the writer
  drops the line.
- A stale id with no row is answered `k,<id>,0.000,dead`.
- An offline id is accepted only if a snapshot of this X-Ray run listed it as a monster or stalker.
- A line whose section differs from the object's now, or whose death was confirmed more than 10 s ago while the id is
  alive again: stale, nothing applied, answered with a `dead` line so the writer drops it. The section compared is
  the object's now (online: the game object's; offline: the alife object's).
- The table is written to a file at the crossing request and read back at the resume, only when the run id matches.
- The actor takes no damage from any of this (standing rule); checked in the witness run.

### 5. When something is lost

| event | ITR2 | X-Ray |
|---|---|---|
| pose writes missed | nothing; every write carries every total | nothing |
| X-Ray stalled, or pose LOST | keeps totals, keeps writing; dead stay dead here | applies nothing; goes on where it was |
| a death unconfirmed through 10 s of snapshots whose `r` is at or past its pose | logs `KILL not applied id=…`, counts it, follows X-Ray | - |
| X-Ray restarts (new run id) | drops every total and open report, logs how many | table gone; world as its save |
| ITR2 restarts (new session number) | totals start at 0 | every `applied` set to 0; damage kept; owed parts dropped |
| a crossing | open reports stay; old level's ids retire by the 60 s rule | table carried over the level change |
| the creature dies in X-Ray first | removes the puppet, drops the line | confirms dead |

The 10 s clock for an unconfirmed death runs only while X-Ray is writing snapshots of our level AND has processed the
pose that carried it, so a stall, a crossing, a restart or a pose X-Ray is not reading cannot trip it.

### 6. Limits to state plainly

- Kills since X-Ray's last save are undone by an X-Ray restart. Accepted for now (section 8, ruling 3).
- An offline kill names no killer: no statistics or relation change for it.
- Hit place (head or body) is not in version 4. X-Ray uses the torso.
- Part hits on a creature that never comes online again are never applied.
- An id reused by another creature of the same section while a line for it is open: the damage goes to the wrong
  creature and neither side can tell. Thought to be very rare (the id pool hands out the longest-free block first).

### 8. RULED by Caden (2026-11-26)

Caden answered the four questions on Discord, 16:50 to 16:54 UTC, as relayed by the Overseer: "You guessed right … Just
itr2". All four as proposed:

1. RULED: the player's shots count by ITR2's health and weapons, not X-Ray's armour and hit zones.
2. RULED: a kill in ITR2 counts as the player's kill in X-Ray (goodwill, squads turning hostile).
3. RULED: a restart of X-Ray undoing the kills since its last save is accepted for now.
4. RULED: loot is in ITR2. The body the player searches is ITR2's; X-Ray's corpse and its loot are not used for it.

### 10. What this amendment does not show (each needs a registered run)
- That a fresh puppet's first health read is its full health, on every class the mod spawns.
- That a health write does not disturb a puppet under ITR2's own AI.
- How long ITR2 keeps a body. The 20-a-second health read assumes longer than 50 ms.
- `STR.kcHealth`'s place in the puppet tick (its 50 ms clock, the order against spawning) is not driven by the offline
  test; the test calls the function directly.
- Everything on X-Ray's side that needs the engine (EnginePerf's list): the direct health change on every class, a
  kill on an object already dying, GAMMA's callbacks on a near-zero hit and on a kill by the actor.
- No bound in this amendment is a timing or a count from a run, so it has no margin table. The 10 s, 60 s, 0.25 s, 50 ms
  and 64 are rules of the design, each checked at its boundary by the offline test (9.9 and 10.05 s; 59.8 and 60.2 s;
  a 300 s stall counted as 0.25 s; 64 lines of 70).
