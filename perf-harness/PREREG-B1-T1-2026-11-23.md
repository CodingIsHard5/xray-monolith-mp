# B1-T1: live one-way puppet link, X-Ray -> ITR2 (JOINT PREREG DRAFT, EnginePerf + ITRZone, 2026-11-23)
Status: DRAFT. Nothing is built or run. The Overseer is asked before the box.
Order (Overseer, after T0 PASS, 2026-11-23): headless X-Ray on Cordon runs the P1 track and exports to tmpfs at 10 Hz,
while ITR2 runs and spawns puppets live. The ITR player's position is NOT fed back (that is T2). No reload or travel.
Register: the latency budget (creature display within 300 ms), the CPU and memory of the two engines on one PC, the
frame time against C5, and the X-Ray stall behaviour (puppets freeze, not vanish).

## 1. What runs
- X-Ray: RefDet14.exe, headless (Xvfb :77), save cordon1, the P1 retry setup (zz_p1_puppet.script: track mover at
  20 Hz, g_god plus refill, the same track file, hash 213ff8f125723f01). The new -b1_snap switch makes the 10 Hz export
  write the live snapshot (section 2) instead of P1's CSV. Nothing else in X-Ray changes. Engine code is untouched:
  it is a gamedata script, installed into the project's own run/ only for the slot, and removed after.
- ITR2: ITRZone's side (section 6), Cordon, reading the snapshot live instead of T0's replay file.
- A Linux-side watcher (section 3) stamps each new snapshot with wall-clock time, and samples CPU and memory.

## 2. Snapshot (X-Ray writer; format CONFIRMED by ITRZone 2026-11-23)
X-Ray rewrites Z:\dev\shm\itrz_b1\snap.txt IN PLACE at each 10 Hz export tick: open "w", write, close.
os.rename is removed from the game's Lua (script_storage.cpp:318-329), so there is no atomic rename. ITR2's reader keeps
a snapshot only when the header seq equals the end line's seq AND is newer than the last one it kept; it rejects empty
files and files with no end line. So a torn file is rejected and the last good snapshot is kept.
  seq,<seq>,<t_xray_ms>,<t_track_s>
  a,<x>,<y>,<z>,<yaw_rad>,<health>
  o,<id>,<section>,<kind m|s>,<x>,<y>,<z>,<yaw_rad>,<health>,<alive 0|1>,<best_enemy_id>,<dist_m>   (one per creature)
  end,<seq>
- Raw X-Ray metres, as in P1. ITR2 does the zone-frame transform and the classes (T0's tables, ported to Lua).
  The 'a' line drives the ITR2 player pose, as in T0. Nothing goes back to X-Ray.
- yaw = atan2(dir.x, dir.z) from o:direction(). alive from o:alive().
- seq starts at 1 and rises by 1 per tick.
- Creature set: the same as P1 (db.storage objects that are monsters or stalkers, i.e. online).
- X-Ray also keeps a meta file (Z:\dev\shm\itrz_b1\meta.csv, as P1's meta): S, ON/OFF, TICK, SUMMARY, plus a
  per-tick line `W,<seq>,<sample_to_close_us>`.

## 3. Measurement (X-Ray and the box)
- Write time: a watcher reads snap.txt every 2 ms and logs `<UTC wall_ms>,<seq>` for each new complete seq
  (header seq == end seq). It also logs torn reads (header != end) as a count.
- Latency = UTC timestamp of ITR2's log line `T1 shown seq=n` - the watcher's written UTC ms for seq n +
  sample_to_close (X-Ray, logged per tick). Neither Lua has a wall clock in ms. ITR2's UE4SS log stamps every line
  from Wine's clock, which is the Linux clock in UTC to 0.1 us, and the watcher logs UTC, so both use one clock.
  ITR2's `T1 accepted seq=n` splits read from apply. Rendering adds up to one more frame (about 16 ms): stated, not
  measured.
- CPU and memory: every 5 s, for each engine process (X-Ray, ITR2) and their wineservers: RSS, RssAnon and CPU % from
  /proc. Also the box total (MemAvailable, swap used, load).
- X-Ray export cost: per tick, from profile_timer (as P1).

## 4. Stall test
At a registered time (track t = 600 s), the driver sends SIGSTOP to the X-Ray engine process for 20 s, then SIGCONT.
This is a real stall: the whole engine freezes, as a hang would.
PASS:
- no puppet is destroyed or hidden during the stall or in the 2 s after it;
- puppets hold their last pose (or ITR2's own AI holds them, per section 6);
- new snapshots resume within 1 s of SIGCONT, and ITR2 accepts them;
- X-Ray itself continues: the track resumes, and there is no crash for the rest of the run.
If X-Ray crashes after SIGCONT, the stall result is still scored on the ITR2 side, and the X-Ray crash is a finding.

## 5. X-Ray checks and predictions
- Snapshot ticks: >= 95 % of the expected 10 Hz ticks are written outside the stall (P1: all).
- Torn reads seen by the watcher: reported, no limit (ITR2 rejects them by design).
- Export cost: mean < 1.0 ms, p99 < 2.0 ms per tick. P1's CSV export was 0.452 / 0.754 ms; the in-place rewrite adds
  an open and close per tick. Prediction 75 %.
- Two engines (JOINT, ITRZone's cap): box MemAvailable never below 8 GB (logged every 5 s from /proc/meminfo), and
  swap growth no more than 1 GB. The box has 60 GB (about 42 GB available) and 16 threads. X-Ray RssAnon was
  3.3-3.6 GB in P1; ITR2 peaked at 6.3 GB in T0 (N6 reference 6.5 GB). Prediction 90 %.
- Latency: median <= 150 ms, p95 <= 300 ms (the order's limit). Prediction 60 %. The desk budget was 160-270 ms with
  an interpolation buffer; ITR2 polls each frame.
- Stall: PASS (with ITR2's registered stale behaviour, section 6). Prediction 70 %. The risk is on the X-Ray side:
  a 20 s SIGSTOP under Wine has not been tried here (a wineserver or audio timeout could crash it after CONT).

## 6. ITR2 side (ITRZone's section)
Agreed so far (ITRZone's message, 2026-11-23); full text to follow when its live reader is registered.
- Reader: strict seq/end, newer-than-last, rejects empty and end-less files. Transform and classes in Lua (from
  t0_snapshots.py): section -> role -> ITR2 class, humans -> MimicAssault.
- Displayed = the frame in which the puppet positions from that seq were set; one log line `T1 shown seq=<n>
  n=<puppets set>` after the sets. Also `T1 accepted seq=<n>`.
- Stale feed: a stale snapshot never destroys or hides a puppet. After 1 s with no new seq, puppets beyond 40 m hold
  their last pose (`T1 stale since seq=n`) and puppets within 40 m keep ITR2's AI. On the first new seq, normal steering
  resumes (`T1 resumed seq=n gap_s=x`). "Not hidden" = no destroy and no SetActorHiddenInGame call of ITR2's own;
  rendering is not visible to the check.
- Frames: p95 and worst reported against C5 (17.97 / 40.11 ms). PASS at p95 <= 25 ms and worst <= 250 ms (the
  stream-check limits, unchanged).
- Its checks, predictions and remaining text: TO COME.

## 7. Slot
One joint EXCLUSIVE slot, asked of the Overseer. One driver holds the claim, and both engines run under it.
About 1,100 s of track (P1), plus boot of both engines, about 30 min.
Stop on a crash of either engine (except an X-Ray crash after the stall, see section 4), a timeout or a failed check.
At most one retry.

## 8. X-Ray side as built (2026-11-23; desk-tested only, nothing run on the box)
- zz_p1_puppet.script -b1_snap (in perf-harness/): export_b1() builds the whole snapshot, then rewrites snap.txt.
  Without -b1_snap, P1 runs exactly as before. Stub test under Lua 5.1: complete snapshots with seq == end, ON/OFF and
  one W line per tick. Snapshots also flow during the 15 s settle before the track starts (t_track < 0); ITR2 may show
  or skip them.
- t1_watch.py: 2 ms poll, UTC ms, complete-new-seq stamps, torn reads (counted per poll, so one torn window gives
  several lines), resources every 5 s (the engine is the biggest-RSS match of its pattern). Tested with a fake 10 Hz
  writer: 30 of 30 seqs stamped, a planted 20 ms torn window caught, resources logged. Its own cost is a 2 ms poll
  loop in Python (a few % of one core), which is in the box CPU figures.
- t1_xray.sh: runs under the joint claim and never claims itself. It installs and removes the script, runs the
  watcher and X-Ray, and performs the stall when snapshot track time >= 600 s (SIGSTOP 20 s, SIGCONT; stall.csv in
  UTC ms). An X-Ray heartbeat failure (seq unchanged 30 s outside the stall) gets a screenshot, thread ticks and a kill.
- Open for the joint driver (ITRZone): the ITR2 process pattern for the watcher, and who holds the claim and launches
  ITR2. Proposed: ITRZone's driver holds the EXCLUSIVE claim with "B1-T1" in its text, starts ITR2, then calls
  tools/t1_xray.sh <out>.
